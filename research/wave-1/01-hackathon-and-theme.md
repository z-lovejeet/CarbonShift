# Wave 1 · IEEE ClimateChain: hackathon, theme, and opportunity constraints

Research cut-off: **16 October 2026**, using the participant's India time context. Purpose: give Wave 2 a defensible foundation for selecting six ideas, then one project. This is a research and decision document, not a project shortlist.

Companion documents: [judges and judging criteria](02-judges-and-judging-criteria.md) · [comparable hackathon winners](03-comparable-hackathon-winners.md).

## 1. What this research establishes

The strongest strategic target is a narrowly defined, understandable environmental problem with a complete user workflow, an independently inspectable evidence trail, and a credible route to physical impact. This is **our interpretation of the supplied brief and rubric**, not an organizer promise about winning. A large user population, elaborate blockchain architecture, or unusual AI model alone does not establish usefulness or impact.

The user wants originality, real problems, substantial impact, broad relevance, everyday accessibility, strict track alignment, and implementation with coding agents. Treat those as design requirements. Treat the published judging criteria as the assessment frame. Do not replace either with guesses about judges' personalities.

No defensible numerical probability of winning can be calculated: competitors' final submissions, judges' scores, weights, and tie-break procedures are unavailable. The appropriate objective is to improve the strength and reliability of the submission across plausible evaluations.

## 3. What the theme means in operational terms

The four tracks are the boundaries. A climate-themed chatbot, generic marketplace, or ecological game cannot qualify merely by mentioning emissions. The project needs a concrete relationship to a track's problem.

COP31 is scheduled for **9–20 November 2026 in Antalya, Türkiye**, independently confirmed by UNFCCC. The hackathon's claim of alignment with COP31 does **not** establish UNFCCC endorsement, guaranteed access to the conference, or an official COP competition. [UNFCCC COP31](https://unfccc.int/cop31).

**Our interpretation of theme fit:** connect a user decision to one of three defensible outcomes: less environmental harm, more effective environmental action, or stronger accountability that enables action. Explain the intermediate steps. Do not count a verified record as an avoided tonne of CO₂.

| Official track | Central problem from supplied brief | Useful everyday connection — inference | A strong prototype would prove — recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbon Markets & Emissions Transparency | Credibility, opaque reporting, double counting, difficult verification | People and small organizations encounter climate claims, receipts, purchases, and emissions information | The origin and limits of a claim can be inspected; a defined duplicate or invalid state is detected/prevented; a useful decision changes |
| Renewable Energy & Energy Trading | Centralized, inefficient energy systems and coordination | Bills, rooftop solar, shared buildings, flexible appliances, charging | A decision or allocation obeys real constraints; consumption/generation evidence is traceable; physical energy and certificate ownership are distinguished |
| Sustainable Supply Chains | Unverifiable sourcing and sustainability claims | Food, packaging, electronics, repair, reuse, returns | A product/material handoff or circular action is recorded and reconciled; claims survive a fraud/error challenge; an actor has a reason to participate |
| Climate Data & Environmental Monitoring | Fragmented, inaccessible, hard-to-verify environmental data | Neighborhood conditions, community observations, local decisions | Data provenance and quality improve a specific decision; missing/stale/contradictory evidence is visible; uncertainty is preserved |

## 4. Track-by-track research implications

### A. Carbon markets and emissions transparency

**Verified background.** UNFCCC distinguishes Article 6.2 accounting/reporting for transferred mitigation outcomes, Article 6.4 crediting, and Article 6.8 non-market cooperation. These are different mechanisms, not interchangeable names for any tokenized carbon app. [UNFCCC Article 6](https://unfccc.int/process-and-meetings/the-paris-agreement/article6). Its Article 6.2 reference manual explains corresponding adjustments as part of avoiding double counting in the applicable international accounting context. [Reference manual](https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/resource/Article_6.2_Reference_Manual.pdf).

**Inference for problem selection.** Separate: duplicate issuance of a project/benefit; duplicate sale of an instrument; reuse after retirement; competing corporate claims; national accounting double claims. A contract may prevent reuse within its own registry while doing nothing about another registry or a physical baseline. State exactly which failure the prototype addresses.

The Berkeley Carbon Trading Project publishes an aggregated voluntary registry database. The inspected page offers **v2026-08**, covers six named registries, provides archives and supporting files, and licenses the database CC BY 4.0. That is a practical starting point for reproducible research, not proof that every suspicious overlap is fraud. [Berkeley database](https://gspp.berkeley.edu/berkeley-carbon-trading-project/offsets-database).

**Novelty warning.** A public project page called CarbonTwin already lists this hackathon and describes cross-registry screening using Berkeley data. Its author describes the results as investigation signals rather than definitive double counting. This is a **team-authored public overlap signal**, not a verified winning entry or confirmation of final eligibility. Do not choose an essentially identical screening product without a substantial, independently checked difference. [CarbonTwin](https://devpost.com/software/carbontwin).

**Selection questions:** Who supplies ground truth? What unique identifier survives registry changes? Can findings be appealed? Can the operator prevent a false accusation? Does the target user have authority to act? Does the claimed benefit depend on registry cooperation unavailable before the deadline?

**Practical metrics:** precision/recall on a labeled duplicate set; review time saved; percentage of records with inspectable source evidence; successful rejection of reuse inside the modeled system. Report those as technical/accountability metrics. Any additional carbon benefit needs a separate causal argument.

### B. Renewable energy and energy trading

**Verified background.** GHG Protocol's Scope 2 Guidance addresses purchased energy, contractual instruments, and quality criteria for market-based accounting; its page also reports a 2025–2026 consultation. A prototype should identify the applicable document/version rather than claim compliance with an assumed newly adopted standard. [Scope 2 Guidance](https://ghgprotocol.org/scope-2-guidance).

**Inference for problem selection.** Distinguish four things: physical electricity delivery, financial settlement, energy attributes/certificates, and estimated emissions. A token transfer demonstrates none of the other three automatically. A market simulation should visibly label synthetic meter data and settlement.

Powerledger already markets blockchain-based peer-to-peer trading and traceability and describes deployments. Electricity Maps already offers electricity data signals through its platform/API. These first-party pages establish strong prior art; their commercial impact claims are not independently audited here. [Powerledger](https://powerledger.io/), [Electricity Maps API](https://www.electricitymaps.com/platform/api).

**Selection questions:** What can a non-solar household do? Who controls a shared meter? Which tariff and grid rules apply? Is this a decision aid, simulated market, or permitted transaction? Can the demo work without utility integrations? Are savings calculated using appropriate local, time-dependent assumptions?

**Practical metrics:** energy shifted under comfort/deadline constraints; forecast error against a simple baseline; reconciliation accuracy; simulated settlement failures prevented; scenario cost and emissions with source/time bounds. Average grid intensity supports an estimate; it does not by itself prove the marginal emissions avoided by shifting a load.

### C. Sustainable supply chains

**Verified background.** UNEP's 2024 report estimates **1.05 billion tonnes of food waste in 2022, including inedible parts**; households accounted for 60%, food services 28%, and retail 12%. Its **8–10% emissions figure concerns food loss and waste together**, not merely the household subset. Do not mix those boundaries. [UNEP report announcement](https://www.unep.org/en/news-and-stories/press-release/world-squanders-over-1-billion-meals-day-un-report).

The Global E-waste Monitor reports **62 million tonnes generated in 2022**, with **22.3% documented as formally collected and recycled**. These are historical global statistics, not 2026 local measurements. [ITU/UNITAR monitor](https://ewastemonitor.info/the-global-e-waste-monitor-2024/).

The European Commission describes digital product passports as progressively introduced through product-specific rules. Its FAQ explicitly rejects the assumption that all products already need a passport; food is excluded from the ESPR framework discussed there. A food traceability prototype may still fit this hackathon, but should not falsely claim a food DPP mandate under ESPR. This is contextual research, not a compliance assessment. [Commission DPP FAQ](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/digital-product-passport/explore-our-faqs_en).

**Inference for problem selection.** Circularity is familiar and relatable, but a scan is not a return, a return is not recycling, and recycling is not necessarily new material displaced. Look for a broken handoff, contested claim, or incentive that the software can credibly improve.

Circularise advertises supply-chain evidence collection, custody/mass-balance tracing, and passports. Recykal advertises QR-based deposit returns and AI-enabled validation. These are existence/feature baselines, not proof of perfect fraud prevention or independently verified performance. [Circularise](https://www.circularise.com/), [Recykal digital DRS](https://www.recykal.com/digital-drs).

**Selection questions:** Who registers an item? Can a copied image/QR earn twice? What evidence establishes weight, custody, reuse, or end processing? Who funds rewards? Why will a shop, repairer, recycler, or collector participate? Does accessibility include low-end phones and people without wallets?

**Practical metrics:** verified material mass at each stage; confirmed handoff rate; duplicate-claim detection; actual repair/reuse completion; measurable reduction in waste for a defined cohort. Estimate CO₂ only when a defensible material/product-specific method supports the comparison.

### D. Climate data and environmental monitoring

**Verified background.** OpenAQ provides an API with key-based access. Climate TRACE provides downloadable emissions data; its terms license emissions data/metadata CC BY 4.0 with external-dataset exceptions. OpenEarth's OpenClimate already addresses interoperable, nested climate accounting using decentralized identifiers and verifiable credentials. A map with blockchain hashes is therefore not an unexplored category. [OpenAQ](https://docs.openaq.org/using-the-api/api-key), [Climate TRACE data](https://climatetrace.org/data), [terms](https://www.climatetrace.org/terms), [OpenClimate](https://www.openearth.org/projects/openclimate).

**Inference for problem selection.** A data product becomes useful when a specific person can make a better decision because of its timeliness, quality, accessibility, or verification. Merely aggregating existing sources creates a dashboard, not demonstrated environmental action.

**Selection questions:** Who acts on the output? What resolution does the decision need? What happens outside sensor coverage? Does a model distinguish absence of data from a normal condition? Can an adversary upload fake readings? Are precise home locations really needed? Does it still work when source data are delayed?

**Practical metrics:** completeness, timeliness, calibration error, disagreement detection, false-alert rate, and user decision time. Air pollutants and greenhouse gases are different quantities: PM2.5 readings do not directly measure CO₂ or establish carbon credits. Environmental monitoring is a named track, so a well-scoped non-CO₂ benefit can still be relevant.

## 6. Technology choices that support the theme

The following are **engineering recommendations** derived from the trust problems in the tracks.

### Give AI an observable job

Suitable jobs include extracting structured evidence from a document, detecting anomalous readings, forecasting a defined quantity, classifying material with uncertainty, or optimizing a constrained decision. For each candidate, specify input, output, baseline, evaluation data, failure mode, and human review route.

An LLM can explain a computed result; it should not invent emission factors, certify unseen disposal, or turn a visual guess into a safety guarantee. Coding agents can accelerate implementation. They do not create representative training data, field validation, permissions, utility partners, or scientific credibility.

### Give blockchain a necessary trust role

Specify the parties, conflicting incentives, shared state, and what a single operator could otherwise change. Credible prototype roles include enforcing a unique claim/retirement, recording multi-party attestations, showing a tamper-evident sequence, or settling a bounded simulated obligation.

Compare against an ordinary database plus signed records. If that simpler alternative solves the actual trust problem equally well, the chain choice needs a clear interoperability/governance argument or the candidate should be reconsidered. This comparison strengthens the proposal even if blockchain is ultimately chosen.

Store sensitive evidence off-chain with appropriate access controls; commit only what needs public verification. A hash shows correspondence with committed bytes, not that the physical event happened. Low-entropy private data may still be guessable through hashes. Define revocation, corrections, dispute resolution, and who is authorized to attest.

### Plan a visible challenge, not only a happy path

In a demo, a user completes an ordinary task; the system provides a useful result; another party can inspect its evidence; then a duplicate, tampered, stale, unauthorized, or uncertain input is handled correctly. This tests whether AI and blockchain actually contribute.

Avoid unnecessary multi-chain deployments, custom tokens, real-money markets, live hardware requirements, or training a large model from scratch unless a chosen problem genuinely depends on them. Given the displayed deadline, those dependencies can weaken the probability of finishing a credible prototype.

## 7. Impact reasoning that survives scrutiny

Use this explicit chain:

**Observed problem → changed decision → completed action → measured environmental result**, with evidence and uncertainty at every transition.

| Claim level | Example of admissible phrasing | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Technical result | The prototype rejected a repeated claim in this test set | Reproducible test, exact conditions, failure cases |
| Operational result | A participant completed a verified return/repair or a review became faster | Actual activity/observations, denominators, comparison |
| Modeled environmental potential | Under stated assumptions, the scenario estimates a reduction | Counterfactual, activity data, factor source/version, geographic/time boundary, uncertainty |
| Measured environmental impact | A defined intervention reduced waste/energy/emissions in this cohort | Suitable measurements and comparison, attribution limits |

Do not present modeled potential as achieved impact. Do not multiply total world waste by an arbitrary adoption percentage and label the result expected savings. Distinguish prevention, reuse, collection, treatment, avoided virgin production, and offsetting.

A useful emissions estimate, **when the chosen method supports it**, is the difference between baseline and intervention emissions, including the intervention's own material/transport/energy costs. Traceability metrics can be valuable without an invented conversion to tonnes.

For accessible products, ask whether the user saves money, time, effort, risk, or gains verifiable value. Climate motivation alone is a fragile adoption assumption. A specific initial community can validate the product while the design remains broadly transferable; it need not launch to everybody simultaneously.

## 8. Wave 2 handoff: research contract

Wave 2 should use all three documents together and produce **six differentiated project candidates**, not six names for the same architecture. A search across all four tracks is required; an equal track quota is not. Every candidate must stay within a named track and the AI/blockchain climate theme.

### Candidate evidence card — fill every field

1. One-sentence problem, a specific initial user, and a concrete moment of need.
2. Primary track; exact challenge addressed; any secondary track relationship.
3. Primary evidence of the problem, with geography/date/boundary; two or more real alternatives and closely related winning projects.
4. Specific novelty: what the user can do or verify that alternatives do not provide in the chosen setting. No unsupported “world first.”
5. A 60-second ordinary-language explanation and a 3–5 minute demonstration outline.
6. AI task, baseline, evaluation set, uncertainty, and fallback.
7. Blockchain parties, state/invariant, oracle boundary, and comparison with a signed centralized system.
8. Data sample already acquired; source/license/resolution; missing fields; offline demonstration fallback.
9. Complete user workflow, evidence/verification step, and one adversarial/error demonstration.
10. Impact chain; prototype metrics; modeled-potential method; what cannot yet be claimed.
11. Initial adoption route, actor incentives, wallet/device friction, external partner dependencies, and scaling bottleneck.
12. Must-build scope, stretch scope, actual time/skills/cost estimate, top three failure risks, and a simpler rescue version.
13. Scores/evidence against all five criteria using document 2; strongest counterargument and proposed test.

### Hard filters before ranking

Reject or rescope candidates with an unclear track, unavailable essential data, an untestable central claim, a chain that cannot establish the promised truth, an external dependency that prevents a complete demo, an unresolved clone-like overlap, or environmental benefits that depend entirely on speculative mass adoption.

Apply the user's accessibility requirement constructively: an ordinary person should understand the problem and benefit quickly; an ordinary participant should have a low-friction entry path; an institutional actor can still perform necessary verification behind that experience. Do not use “everyone” to hide who actually acts first.

### Recommended selection procedure — internal, not official

Start with a wider problem search, then retain six candidates after prior-art and data checks. Score each on the five published criteria, record uncertainty separately, and challenge the top contenders with an independent skeptical review. Test their ordering under impact-heavy, execution-heavy, and balanced assumptions. Select only after proving the riskiest dependency with a small real data or workflow experiment.

For planning against the displayed close, reserve the final day for deployment, video, documentation, and submission. This is a scheduling recommendation; obtain clarification of the conflicting rules before relying on any specific submission interpretation.
