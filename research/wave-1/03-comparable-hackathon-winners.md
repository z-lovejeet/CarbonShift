# Wave 1 · Document 3: Verified comparable hackathon winners

**Research date:** 16 October 2026.  
**Purpose:** Give Wave 2 a defensible precedent library, a map of already-used mechanisms, and an evidence standard for selecting six ClimateChain concepts. This is research, not the six-idea shortlist.

## 1. What the evidence supports

This corpus contains **14 verified prize-winning projects**, spanning carbon accountability, energy, circular consumption, and environmental data. It deliberately distinguishes an overall place, a named global category award, a sustainability track prize, and a sponsor prize. Those are different outcomes. A sponsor pool prize is particularly weak evidence of superiority over an entire hackathon field.

The strongest transferable conclusion is **to choose a specific environmental decision, implement the consequential workflow, and make its evidence inspectable**. That is our synthesis, not a published explanation of why these judges selected these projects. There is no defensible numerical winning probability for a future ClimateChain idea in these sources.

ClimateChain itself asks for alignment with one of four tracks and evaluates climate impact, innovation, execution, usefulness, and communication. Use its actual rubric to choose a project; previous awards provide precedents rather than a replacement scoring system. [Official ClimateChain brief](https://ieee-climatechain-hack.devpost.com/)

### Evidence labels used below

- **Award verified:** An organizer, official competition platform, or organizer-maintained showcase records the exact result.
- **Artifact inspected:** A public repository, policy contribution, or specific source file was read. This establishes what was published, not that its system works in production.
- **Team-reported:** Functionality, partnerships, savings, or deployment described by the authors. These are not independently validated outcomes.
- **Analysis:** Our judgment about transfer, adoption friction, or the next question to investigate.

**Scope limit:** No project was installed, run end to end, security-audited, or independently field-tested in this research. Repository observations refer to their accessible state on the research date; a modern default branch can contain changes made after the award. Event submissions are the better source for what a team claimed at submission time.

## 3. Project case studies

### W01 · Agrify: carbon finance for regenerative farming

**Problem and user:** Farmers and carbon-project developers need credible evidence for agricultural carbon claims. The team proposed satellite data and machine learning combined with Hedera Guardian to connect farm practices to carbon finance. NASA and Nigeria's NASRDA are named data sources in its submission. [Original submission](https://devpost.com/software/agrify-generating-vcus-through-regenerative-agriculture)

**What is evidenced:** A Guardian methodology contribution was actually merged on 9 April 2024. That is a concrete deliverable beyond a promotional description. Its public record does not establish independently audited soil-carbon accuracy, registry-issued credits, or farmer revenue. A university pilot and institutional relationships are team-reported. [Merged methodology contribution](https://github.com/hashgraph/guardian/pull/3492), [Submission claims](https://devpost.com/software/agrify-generating-vcus-through-regenerative-agriculture)

**Analysis for Wave 2:** Transfer the clear economic beneficiary and auditable methodology. Do not inherit a promise that satellite analysis alone validates sequestration. Check access to field truth, uncertainty, baseline, additionality, permanence, and credit issuance authority before attempting this class. A buildable interface is much easier than a scientifically defensible carbon claim.

### W02 · Omnii: environmental-data oracle for carbon projects

**Problem and user:** Carbon-project verification is slow and difficult to inspect. The submission links environmental measurements to project-verification certificates: Polygon ERC-721 records, IPFS metadata, a Python service, and a dashboard. No separate AI capability is established merely by calling this an oracle. [Original showcase and prize](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/omnii-urkyn)

**What is evidenced:** The published code retrieves Verra project information and dClimate biomass rasters. The inspected implementation uses historical years **2010, 2017, and 2018**; this narrows the submission's broad “real-time” framing. It is a prototype data-processing path, not independent evidence that carbon methodologies have been automated correctly. [Pinned carbon-data implementation](https://github.com/OliverT1/omnii/blob/b60f59081c7ce1c45deb8b6f603c213c98a8dc7f/server/carbon_density.py)

**Analysis for Wave 2:** Transfer the trace from project ID to measurement to signed record. Do not copy broad replacement-of-verifiers claims. A narrower anomaly or evidence-checking tool may be testable where automatic credit certification is not. Satellite + blockchain carbon verification is already a prize-winning precedent, so that combination alone is not novel.

### W03 · mundo: making carbon retirement usable across chains

**Problem and user:** Existing on-chain carbon instruments were awkward for users holding assets on other chains. mundo routes payment and retirement through Hyperlane, with Chainlink price normalization and Toucan integration. The team reported terminals on Polygon, Celo, and Optimism. AI was not part of the described mechanism. [Original showcase](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/mundo-wh4t0)

**What is evidenced:** The repository contains carbon-settlement contracts and deployment artifacts. This supports a technical prototype; it does not independently validate underlying credit quality or prove additional climate benefit from easier retirement. [Source repository](https://github.com/clague17/ethsf22)

**Analysis for Wave 2:** Transfer a completed action with a receipt, rather than stopping at a dashboard. Do not assume multi-chain complexity matters to an everyday user. This pattern requires wallet use, liquidity, dependable bridges, and credible credits. It is stronger as a solution to a known carbon-market workflow than as a universal daily-life climate product.

### W04 · Lowcarb: carbon-aware federated-learning scheduling

**Problem and user:** Distributed AI training has location-dependent electricity emissions. Lowcarb changes which Flower clients participate in a round using carbon forecasts while considering participation fairness. Here AI training is the workload being optimized, not a chatbot placed on top of an unrelated application. No blockchain role is described. [Original submission](https://taikai.network/gsf/hackathons/carbonhack22/projects/cl8wyghoi75233101wux9vigobp/idea)

**What is evidenced:** There is a published package, strategy implementation, tutorial, and backtesting material. The organizer reports a **13% emissions reduction in its use case without an accuracy penalty**. Treat this as a reported experiment, not a general guarantee for all datasets or grids. [Repository](https://github.com/birnbaum/flwr-lowcarb), [Organizer report](https://greensoftware.foundation/articles/carbonhack22-a-big-leap-in-carbon-aware-computing/)

**Analysis for Wave 2:** Transfer automatic behavior change, a baseline comparison, and a user cost constraint. The project's specialist audience is evidence that universal consumer appeal is not a prerequisite for recognition in every event. ClimateChain would still need an explicit emissions-transparency or energy-system fit and a justified ledger role if proposed.

### W05 · Carbon-Aware Zeus: reduce emissions without abandoning the user's task

**Problem and user:** Training operators cannot always relocate or postpone jobs. The submission adjusts GPU power limits in response to carbon intensity and reports a ResNet50/ImageNet experiment using one NVIDIA A40 GPU. It uses forecasts and optimization; no blockchain is described. [Original submission](https://taikai.network/en/gsf/hackathons/carbonhack22/projects/cl95qxjpa70555701uhg96r0ek6/idea)

**What is evidenced:** The authors report **24% less carbon with 3% longer training** in that experiment. The organizer recap instead says learning time decreased 3%. Use the original submission's direction and flag the conflict; do not repeat the recap's wording as an established result. Carbon-awareness was an extension of existing Zeus work, with further polishing planned. [Original experimental account](https://taikai.network/en/gsf/hackathons/carbonhack22/projects/cl95qxjpa70555701uhg96r0ek6/idea), [Conflicting organizer recap](https://greensoftware.foundation/articles/carbonhack22-a-big-leap-in-carbon-aware-computing/)

**Analysis for Wave 2:** Transfer the explicit trade-off: environmental gain and user inconvenience measured together. Do not transfer the percentage to a different application. This is a useful evaluation pattern even if the eventual project concerns food, household energy, or recycling.

### W06 · Nodebility: simulated waste-to-bioenergy accountability

**Problem and user:** The project connects organic-waste processing, energy access, and carbon evidence for communities and project operators. AI agents analyze a Bionode data stream; Hedera Guardian, tokens, and governance connect operational information to actions. [Organizer result](https://hedera.com/blog/these-are-the-winners-of-the-hello-future-origins-hackathon/), [Team repository](https://github.com/chboey/nodebility)

**What is evidenced:** Its own roadmap distinguishes a completed **simulator** from forthcoming physical pilots. The inspected simulator generates scenarios and invokes mint analysis when simulated cumulative output reaches 100 kWh; that call alone does not establish a successful token mint. The README equates a BIOGAS token with a tonne of avoided CO₂e, but this research found no independent validation of that conversion or registry certification. Do not present the prototype as deployed villages producing certified credits. [Roadmap and token claims](https://github.com/chboey/nodebility), [Pinned simulator](https://github.com/chboey/nodebility/blob/b12ffbd7d6212fd9e682729bdb93d27a52ece9a5/backend/utils/simulator.ts)

**Analysis for Wave 2:** Transfer a visible physical-to-digital workflow and clearly labeled simulation. Do not assume a ledger makes a sensor trustworthy or a generated token a recognized offset. Hardware operation, calibration, maintenance, feedstock, and emissions methodology create adoption work that coding agents cannot remove.

### W07 · PowerAmp: decentralized energy-demand management

**What is verified:** Hedera's results identify Tom Ploem's PowerAmp as the sustainability-track winner and describe it as a decentralized demand-management platform on Hedera. [Organizer results](https://hedera.com/blog/the-winning-projects-of-the-beyond-blockchain-hackathon-2023/)

**Evidence gap:** The researched organizer article provides no detailed meter integration, AI description, baseline experiment, usable repository, deployment proof, or consumer onboarding flow. Accordingly, it would be misleading to invent a peer-to-peer electricity exchange or an AI optimizer for this case. Intended energy participants are a reasonable audience inference, not a verified product specification.

**Analysis for Wave 2:** This establishes an award precedent for demand management, not a ready implementation recipe. Evaluate any related concept against real meter data, the decision maker who can shift demand, settlement boundaries, and local operating constraints. If those cannot be demonstrated, a convincing energy-market visual can overstate how much of the system exists.

### W08 · Greentrace: scan a product and inspect its sustainability record

**Problem and user:** Shoppers cannot easily inspect product sustainability claims; certifiers and businesses need a way to publish supporting records. The submission describes barcode scanning and a two-sided record-registration workflow using Hedera Guardian. AI is not a documented central component. [Original submission](https://devpost.com/software/greentrace-3oxjig)

**What is evidenced:** The public repository contains a scanner interface, policy schema, and server code. An inspected server file implements a TinyDB key/value store; that file alone is not proof of complete Guardian verification. The award and published prototype do not prove that real product claims were independently checked or that shopping behavior changed. [Repository](https://github.com/cbonoz/greentrace), [Pinned server implementation](https://github.com/cbonoz/greentrace/blob/c9a1456b34afb675e177f065d8eb4ae19f52f3fe/server/main.py)

**Analysis for Wave 2:** Transfer the familiar scan interaction and separate contributor/consumer roles. Do not stop at a barcode lookup that repeats brand statements. The harder questions are evidence authority, revocation, missing items, copied labels, conflicting certificates, and why a producer will supply accurate data. A generic sustainability scanner has direct winning prior art.

### W09 · SusEats: near-expiry food auctions

**Problem and user:** Restaurants have food approaching expiry; nearby buyers can use it. SusEats combines listings and auctions with an AI ranking system using preferences, budget, and location. The original submission explicitly describes Solidity contracts running on **local Ganache**, not a public-chain production deployment. [Original project and award record](https://devpost.com/software/suseats)

**What is evidenced:** The repository includes auction contracts and application code. No independently measured avoided food waste, operating restaurant network, or sustained customer adoption was established here. [Source repository](https://github.com/olsenbudanur/SusEats)

**Analysis for Wave 2:** Transfer a benefit people already want—affordable food—and a complete seller-to-buyer transaction. Do not reproduce the old submission's claim of uniqueness. Pickup reliability, food safety, inventory truth, and enough local suppliers are more important to adoption than a blockchain badge. A new food-waste project needs a distinct unresolved failure, not just another marketplace with AI recommendations.

### W10 · EcoTrack: AI scoring and attestations for daily environmental actions

**Problem and user:** Individuals receive little visible feedback or reward for sustainable behavior. EcoTrack accepts action submissions, scores them with Gaia AI, and records attestations with Verax; World ID and a MetaMask interface are also described. The authors themselves qualify the AI node as **currently centralized**. [Original showcase and prizes](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/ecotrack-0z7id)

**What is evidenced:** The repository contains an event handler that requests a model-generated score and writes it back to a contract. This is evidence of a scoring integration, not validated environmental measurement, robust image authenticity, or proof that behavior was additional. [Pinned scoring integration](https://github.com/kalashshah/public-goods/blob/cb53dff487e2505f21f0556184853bad2b110a69/functions/index.ts)

**Analysis for Wave 2:** Transfer easy participation and visible receipts only when the underlying claim can be checked. An image can be reused, staged, or unrelated to a net environmental benefit; human uniqueness does not establish action truth. Reward economics, verifier error, privacy, and ongoing funding need answers. “Photo → AI green score → token reward” is already demonstrated prior art.

### W11 · WaterWise: satellite information becomes an irrigation schedule

**Problem and user:** Farmers need watering decisions rather than another technical data feed. WaterWise maps fields and computes a seven-day schedule using OpenET evapotranspiration, Open-Meteo precipitation, and crop coefficients. Its SproutAI chatbot uses GPT-3.5 Turbo; the scheduling calculation should not be misrepresented as being entirely driven by an LLM. No blockchain mechanism is described. [Original team submission](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/gaamaramma/?tab=project)

**What is evidenced:** The submission provides a project and demo link. Persistent farm storage and notifications appear among future goals. No controlled field experiment proving water savings or crop-yield improvements was established. [Submission and roadmap](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/find-a-team/gaamaramma/?tab=project)

**Analysis for Wave 2:** Transfer the move from observation to a concrete, time-bound action. Do not assume dataset coverage, resolution, crop suitability, or forecast accuracy in the intended pilot location. This is broad human relevance with a specific initial user, rather than an app claiming to serve everyone equally.

### W12 · G.R.O.W.: conversational access to wildfire observations

**Problem and user:** Environmental information can be inaccessible to people unfamiliar with GIS. The current Team I.O. repository describes a map and voice interface using NASA FIRMS observations, weather context, and an OpenAI assistant with tools. Blockchain is not described as part of that workflow. [Team README](https://github.com/Florianopolis-NASA-Space-Apps/wildfires_chat_dashboard/blob/main/README.md)

**What is evidenced:** Published application materials explain data ingestion and queries. Its README uses a “top-10 finalist” description, but **NASA's result confirms a global Local Impact award**; use the organizer for status. Present-day implementation details are not automatically the exact 2024 build. No operational emergency-service certification or independently measured losses avoided was found. [NASA result](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-2024-global-winners/), [Current repository](https://github.com/Florianopolis-NASA-Space-Apps/wildfires_chat_dashboard/blob/main/README.md)

**Analysis for Wave 2:** Transfer natural-language access to real structured observations, with source and timestamp visible. Distinguish observed hotspots, estimated risk, and predictions. Any consequential alert needs failure handling, latency disclosure, geographic coverage, and a clear user action. Adding a voice assistant alone is insufficient differentiation.

### W13 · Eco-Metropolis: understandable consequences through play

**Problem and user:** Students need an approachable way to explore sustainability decisions. The team describes age-based city-planning and resource-management scenarios using environmental data. AI and blockchain are not established core mechanisms in the inspected artifact. [Team repository](https://github.com/j-shafeeqa/Eco-Metropolis-Sustainable-City-Simulation---NASA-Space-App)

**What is evidenced:** The accessible repository contains a detailed README and a Python quest file, with presentation and wireframe links. That is not evidence of an implemented full game matching every described quest. The inspirational award is real, but educational intent does not establish measured emissions reduction. [Repository artifacts](https://github.com/j-shafeeqa/Eco-Metropolis-Sustainable-City-Simulation---NASA-Space-App), [NASA award confirmation](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-2024-global-winners/)

**Analysis for Wave 2:** Transfer a comprehensible decision, consequence, and feedback loop. Do not assume a sustainability game fits ClimateChain without a substantive connection to one of its four tracks. Use this as presentation research; it is weaker evidence for the eligibility of an education-only concept.

### W14 · Carboni: hourly renewable-energy certificate workflow

**Problem and user:** Renewable producers and energy buyers need traceable generation evidence. Carboni proposes hourly production/consumption matching using Guardian credentials and tokenized energy attributes. AI is not an established core component. [Original submission repository](https://github.com/amir-gerges/Carboni-Hedera-africa-hackathon-submission)

**What is evidenced:** Public policy, schema, whitepaper, and pitch artifacts exist. The README explicitly says its working flow ends at **device onboarding**; operator meter-data processing, trading approvals, fractionalization, and retirement are future work. This is evidence of a bounded policy prototype, not a deployed meter-to-retirement service. [Pinned submission README](https://github.com/amir-gerges/Carboni-Hedera-africa-hackathon-submission/blob/64265331456fa3d551f708e700e88dc3bdcd9d07/README.md)

**Analysis for Wave 2:** Transfer temporal matching and participant accountability. Do not treat “offset” terminology as proof of recognized carbon offsets or physical electricity delivery. A stronger demonstrator should expose the complete evidence lifecycle and its failure cases. Meter access, duplicate certificates, data authority, and participant adoption remain key questions.

## 5. What transfers to judging—and what cannot be inferred

The following are **design and evaluation recommendations** derived from comparing artifacts with ClimateChain's rubric. They are not published judge explanations, weights, or guarantees.

| ClimateChain criterion | Useful evidence to create in Wave 2/implementation | What the precedents caution against |
|---|---|---|
| Climate impact | A causal chain from user action to environmental outcome; units; a baseline; conservative assumptions; separate observed and modeled results | Reporting tokens, scores, users, map markers, or transactions as tonnes of avoided emissions |
| Innovation | Name the nearest existing mechanism and the exact unresolved failure being solved | Calling the combination “AI + blockchain” unique; presenting old winner ideas as unexplored |
| Technical execution | A reproducible path from input to useful decision to verifiable record; a meaningful failure case | Counting integrations, using a marketing description as proof, or masking simulation |
| Practical usefulness | A first user with an immediate reason to adopt; an accessible input; tolerable inconvenience; plausible partner requirements | Promising everyone as a user while depending on unavailable producers, auditors, meters, or hardware |
| Presentation and communication | One familiar situation, one consequential action, one observable result, and a short evidence trail | Spending the demo explaining chain architecture before demonstrating a human benefit |

### Five specific lessons

1. **Specificity can coexist with broad relevance.** Farmers, restaurant buyers, product shoppers, and energy participants face concrete decisions. A product can expand from one of these without claiming that everyone needs the same interface.
2. **Consumer reach and climate magnitude are separate dimensions.** A frequently used consumer feature can have tiny or unverified impact; a narrow operational improvement can affect a large emissions source. Evaluate both, without assuming either automatically wins.
3. **Integrity of storage and truth of inputs are separate.** An immutable record may preserve a false sensor value, copied photo, unsupported producer statement, or inaccurate model estimate. Show where trust enters the system and how a claim can be challenged or corrected.
4. **A working prototype can honestly use simulation.** Nodebility illustrates why a simulated physical process should be labeled. Demonstrate software behavior while making the missing physical evidence explicit.
5. **A quantified comparison is stronger evidence than an ambitious impact sentence.** Lowcarb and Zeus provide reported baselines and trade-offs. Their numbers remain experiment-specific; the transferable asset is the evaluation structure.

## 7. Wave 2 research brief

### Selection requirements for each of the six future concepts

Before describing a concept as a high-quality contender, prepare the following evidence card. These are proposed internal gates, not additional official eligibility rules.

1. **One primary official track**, with the exact challenge addressed. Secondary benefits may exist, but should not make the core problem ambiguous.
2. **A one-sentence everyday problem**, plus one initial user and one payer/operator where relevant. Explain how it could reach more people after that first use case works.
3. **A new mechanism or unresolved failure**, compared with at least two relevant precedents here and current non-hackathon alternatives researched separately.
4. **A causal environmental benefit**, with an honest unit, baseline, assumptions, and an uncertainty statement. Avoid converting unverifiable behavior into offsets.
5. **A necessary role for AI**, if used: what input it processes, what error matters, what the baseline is, and why a simpler method is insufficient.
6. **A necessary role for blockchain**, if used: which parties must share trust, what state or claim is recorded, what can be revoked/disputed, and why a conventional database alone would not meet that particular need.
7. **A real-data entry point today**, including source, coverage, licensing/access constraints, update interval, and a permitted fallback dataset. “We will obtain partner data” is a dependency, not evidence.
8. **One consequential demo**, achievable with the actual remaining time: input → decision/action → receipt/evidence. Label every simulated boundary.
9. **An adoption test**, such as a potential user's response to a working workflow or a measured reduction in steps/time. A huge theoretical audience is not an adoption result.
10. **A build-risk register**, separating tasks coding agents can accelerate from scientific validation, equipment, institutional access, and external authorization.

### How to compare the six fairly

Use the five official judging dimensions and record evidence confidence beside every internal score. Keep **official criteria** separate from **our preferences**: broad daily-life use, ease of explanation, and agent-assisted buildability are useful selection factors requested by the user, but are not published scoring weights. Do not invent judge-specific probabilities or turn a subjective score into a percentage chance of winning.

Apply a sensitivity check: would the ranking change if technical execution mattered more, if the user-base estimate were halved, or if a partner/data dependency failed? Prefer concepts whose case remains credible across those variations. Reject unsupported impact before optimizing the pitch.

### Important research still needed in Wave 2

- **Current competition and commercial alternatives:** this is a winner library, not proof of worldwide novelty.
- **Physical electricity trading comparisons:** W14 supplies a direct REC precedent, but its documented implementation boundary does not establish live delivery or a complete certificate lifecycle. Investigate operational and registry alternatives separately.
- **Problem evidence in the chosen geography:** verify the real bottleneck, current workflow, and plausible first user.
- **Dataset viability:** test access and sample records before relying on an appealing source name.
- **Ground truth and evaluation:** choose what can be measured within the build window and what remains a future pilot.

## 8. Biases, exclusions, and source conflicts

**Winner-only bias:** These projects were selected because they won an identifiable award and had usable public sources. We did not compare the complete losing fields, so the corpus cannot identify causal features that distinguish winners from non-winners.

**Sponsor bias:** Blockchain events reward particular infrastructure use. A sponsor prize may reflect the sponsor's bounty more than overall climate value. Do not optimize ClimateChain around Hyperlane, Toucan, Gaia, or Hedera simply because those projects won their sponsors' categories.

**Event bias:** CarbonHack22 was about carbon-aware software; NASA Space Apps recognizes distinct global categories and uses NASA/partner resources; DLT Climate emphasized digital methodologies. Those objectives differ from ClimateChain's four-track brief. [CarbonHack organizer](https://greensoftware.foundation/articles/carbonhack22-a-big-leap-in-carbon-aware-computing/), [NASA award process](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/nasa-space-apps-2024/awards/), [DLT Climate organizer](https://www.dltearth.com/news/dlt-climate-hackathon-winners-event-highlights)

**Publicity is not deployment:** Awards establish competition recognition. A source repository establishes published material. Neither proves long-term uptake, independently verified climate outcomes, production security, or valid carbon-credit issuance.

**Availability bias:** English-language, online, software-heavy examples are easier to inspect. This corpus should not be treated as an exhaustive global ranking or a representative sample of all climate hackathons.

**Excluded from the evidence base:** Unverified “winning” posts, finalist-only projects without an actual prize, secondary listicles as proof of awards, and claimed judge-selection reasons without a primary explanation. No verified closely matched IEEE winner with sufficient primary award and technical evidence was added merely to create an IEEE label.

**Recorded conflicts:** Zeus training-time direction differs between organizer recap and original submission; the original experiment is used. G.R.O.W.'s README understates its status as finalist; NASA's named award controls. Several Space Apps project pages intermittently failed to fetch, and some current award-list views returned unrelated team lists; NASA's dated announcement controls the award register. These access problems do not erase confirmed awards, but limit inspection of submissions.
