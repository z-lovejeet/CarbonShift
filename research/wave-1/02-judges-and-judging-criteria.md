## 2. The official rubric and what it does not say

The **five criterion names and their scope** below come from the user's supplied event brief and were cross-checked against the [official overview](https://ieee-climatechain-hack.devpost.com/) and [rules](https://ieee-climatechain-hack.devpost.com/rules). The pages differ slightly in the presentation criterion's label but describe the same task: clearly communicate problem, solution, and environmental impact.

**No numeric weights, scoring scale, tie-break rule, individual judge assignment, or detailed judging-round protocol was found in the inspected pages.** Equal weighting below is an internal comparison device, not an official scoring rule. Do not describe any judge as the sole technical, climate, or commercial evaluator.

| Published criterion | Meaning from the supplied brief | Evidence to design into a strong entry — our recommendation | What weakens a claim — our analysis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Climate Impact** | Addresses a real environmental challenge and improves relevant outcomes or accountability | Sourced problem; explicit intervention pathway; sensible counterfactual; measured prototype metrics; transparent modeled potential | Counting tokens/users/scans as CO₂ saved; no action after an alert; unsupported global extrapolation |
| **Innovation & Creativity** | A fresh, useful application within a track | Closest prior-art comparison; one concrete new capability or underserved constraint; a demo that makes the difference visible | A familiar dashboard with new branding; calling all blockchain applications novel; claiming a world first without evidence |
| **Technical Execution** | Appropriate technology that works as intended | Complete core workflow; real input; evaluated AI task; genuine contract/ledger behavior; error/adversarial demonstration; reproducible setup | Mocked central mechanism presented as working; unexplained AI output; untested permissions; fragile live dependency |
| **Practical Usefulness** | Realistic adoption, scalability, and benefit for actual users/organizations | Named first user and moment of need; low-friction flow; actor incentives; deployment/operating costs; integration and scaling plan | “Everyone will use it”; external partners assumed but unavailable; wallet/token friction; rewards with no funding |
| **Presentation & Communication / Storytelling** | Clear demo of problem, functioning solution, and environmental value | Ordinary-language opening; one cohesive live/replayable journey; inspectable evidence; honest achieved-versus-projected results | Feature tour without a user; jargon before purpose; unsupported impact numbers; ambitious roadmap replacing actual functionality |

### Translate the rubric into a 3–5 minute evidence story

This is a suggested structure, not a compulsory organizer format:

1. **0:00–0:30:** a recognizable user, concrete environmental problem, and one sourced fact.
2. **0:30–1:00:** today's failed workflow and the precise change the prototype enables.
3. **1:00–2:30:** complete user action, bounded AI task, meaningful ledger/contract behavior, and resulting decision/action.
4. **2:30–3:15:** challenge the system with a duplicate, tampered, stale, unauthorized, or uncertain input.
5. **3:15–4:00:** show actual evaluation and evidence; separate measured results from modeled potential.
6. **4:00–4:45:** adoption, operating cost, scaling bottleneck, limitations, and next validation step.

For a shorter video, compress proportionally. Put the user-visible result early. A nontechnical viewer should understand the value while a technical judge can inspect the evidence.

## 3. Internal Wave 2 scorecard — deliberately separate from official rules

First apply the hard filters in document 1. A non-eligible, off-track, or unbuildable idea cannot be rescued by a high numerical score.

Score **each of the five official dimensions from 0 to 5**, using a short written justification and linked evidence. Balanced total: **0–25**. The numbers organize discussion; they are not calibrated to judge scores or winning odds.

| Internal score | Evidence anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Criterion is missing or contradicted by the proposal |
| 1 | An assertion or attractive concept with almost no supporting evidence |
| 2 | Plausible mechanism, but a central assumption remains untested |
| 3 | Specific, coherent proposal with credible sources and a feasible demonstration plan |
| 4 | Strong differentiation and a representative working experiment/validation supporting the criterion |
| 5 | Compelling evidence for the chosen scope, with relevant comparison, limitations, and failure cases addressed |

Apply the anchors **within the criterion**. A working interface does not earn a high climate-impact score; user interviews do not substitute for contract execution. At idea-selection time, mark scores as provisional and attach the experiment needed to increase confidence.

Keep a separate table of **critical assumptions**: confidence (high/medium/low), consequence if false, test, cost/time, and fallback. Do not camouflage a low-confidence assumption by multiplying it by an invented numerical confidence factor.

### Robustness checks for the six candidates

- Compare the balanced ordering with an **impact-heavy scenario** (40% impact, 15% each of the other four criteria) and an **execution-heavy scenario** (40% execution, 15% each of the other four). These are hypothetical stress tests, not inferred official weights.
- Prefer contenders that remain strong under all three views and lack a fatal dependency. Inspect the weakest criterion instead of hiding it in an average.
- Keep a candidate on the shortlist when a small experiment could resolve its uncertainty. Downgrade or rescope one whose essential proof requires unavailable partners, physical infrastructure, or long-term measurement.
- If two ideas are close, run the most discriminating experiment: actual data availability, a verified multi-party handoff, a user explanation test, a benchmark, or a core fraud-prevention invariant.
- Do not add a numeric “judge fit” bonus based on employer, country, title, apparent interests, or association with a platform. Use expertise to prepare scrutiny questions and prior-art comparisons.

### Broad users and uniqueness: operational definitions

**Broad relevance:** many people recognize the problem and can plausibly benefit as the system spreads. **Initial adoption:** a specific group can complete the workflow now. **Everyday accessibility:** ordinary participants can use their existing device without learning blockchain terminology. The user wants all three; none requires claiming immediate global adoption.

**Useful novelty:** a specific action, verification, incentive, or constraint is handled materially better than close alternatives. It may be a new combination, workflow, or underserved setting. Merely adding AI or blockchain to an existing product is insufficient. Each of the six ideas needs a named closest alternative and a falsifiable difference.

## 4. Evidence conventions for the dossiers

- **Organizer-listed / organizer-confirmed:** the event publishes a role. This confirms the event's representation, not every external credential independently.
- **Institutionally corroborated / independently supported:** a separate employer, university, IEEE, publisher, or official record supports the fact. An institutional CV can still be self-maintained or stale.
- A **scheduled talk** is a program entry, not proof of delivery. A **publication** establishes authorship and studied subject matter, not an endorsement or independently reproduced result.
- **Inference** and preparation questions are our recommendations, not quotations or private intentions.
- **Unresolved** means evidence is insufficient or conflicts. It does not mean a fact is false, and it must not be silently replaced with a plausible biography.
- Research is limited to relevant public professional information. No personal contacts, sensitive details, or psychological profiling are needed.
